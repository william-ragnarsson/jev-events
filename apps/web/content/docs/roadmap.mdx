Jev Events is young, and the core API will change before 1.0. Each change is listed in the changelog.

## Available

- **Core**: `listen()`, typed events, policies and review bands, dry-run, protected users, budgets, rate limits, stale dropping, the text cache, `burst()`, `logTo()` and `mockJev()`.
- **Sources**: `from()`, `webhook()`, and the read-only public Twitch chat and Bluesky firehose.
- **Gmail and Google Calendar**: new mail with trash, archive, label, star, mark read and draft reply; new and changed events with accept, decline and maybe. Protected by default, and nothing is ever deleted or sent. Checked by polling, with your own Google Cloud project, so your mail never passes through anyone else's servers.
- **Slack**: messages in the channels and DMs your app is in, over Socket Mode or the Events API, with reply, react and post.
- **Twitch**: anonymous and signed-in chat, `timeout`, `ban`, `deleteMessage`, `warn`, `reply`, `say` and `clip`, and device-code sign-in.
- **CLI**: `watch` for every source, and `auth google`, `auth slack` and `auth twitch`.
- **Benchmarks**: a labeled chat dataset and an open runner.

## In development

In this order:

1. **Monitors per connection**: `monitor()` replaces `listen()`. You write a monitor once, and it runs for every user who connects an account. It runs inside your web app as a webhook route and a cron route, or as a long-running worker, and keeps tokens and sync state in your Postgres database.
2. **Push for Gmail and Google Calendar**: Google tells your app about new mail and events instead of being checked every few seconds. Push needs a public URL, so it comes with monitors.
3. **Outlook mail and calendar**, through Microsoft Graph, with the same actions.

Each integration ships with its own guide.

## Planned

Discord, YouTube, Linear, Google Drive, Microsoft Teams and GitHub. Demand sets the order, so tell us which one you need.
