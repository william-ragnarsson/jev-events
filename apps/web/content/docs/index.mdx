## What it does

Jev Events is an open-source TypeScript library. It watches a stream, such as an inbox, a calendar, a Slack workspace or a Twitch chat, asks [TypeSafe's Jev](https://docs.typesafe.ai) your questions about each new item, and turns the answers into events. You handle each event with a built-in action, such as archiving an email or timing out a chatter, or with your own code.

<Snippet name="intro" title="monitor.ts" mark='google.gmail.inbox()|kind: recipes.email.kind|"kind:newsletter"|google.gmail.archive()' />

<Rows>

- **Source** `google.gmail.inbox()` emits each new email. `slack.messages()`, `twitch.chat()`, a webhook or any async iterable work the same way, and each item has typed fields for its platform, such as an email's `subject`.
- **Questions** `recipes.email.kind` is a `choice` between labels. A question can also be a yes/no `noul`, or a `score` on your own scale. All of an item's questions go to Jev in one request.
- **Events** Names come from your questions, so `"kind:newsletter"` exists only because `kind` has a `newsletter` label. A typo is a compile error.
- **Actions** `google.gmail.archive()` only logs what it would do until you turn dry-run off, and never touches protected people, such as colleagues. Your own functions run as soon as an answer fires.

</Rows>

## Two ways to run it

| | On your machine | For your users |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whose account | Yours, signed in once with `npx jev-events auth` | Each user's, connected from your app |
| Runs as | A script you start, such as `npx tsx monitor.ts` | Routes in your web app, plus a worker for chat |
| Tokens are kept | In `.jev-events/` next to your code | In your Postgres database, encrypted |
| Start with | [**Quickstart →**](/docs/quickstart) | [**For your users →**](/docs/your-users) |

Each integration's page, such as [Gmail](/docs/integrations/gmail), has a builder that writes the code for either one. To see what Jev says before writing anything, `npx jev-events watch` prints its answers in your terminal. See [the CLI](/docs/cli).

## Where your data goes

Jev Events is a library, not a service. It runs in your own process or web app, with your own TypeSafe key and your own apps on each platform. Items go to TypeSafe's API to be judged, actions go straight to the platform, and tokens stay on your machine or in your database.
